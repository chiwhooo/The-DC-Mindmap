We open from the perspective of [[Harold 'Hal' Jordan]], a [[Green Lantern]] and also one of Barry's best friends. He's commenting on the current mental state of [[Bartholomew 'Barry' Allen]] and how he's been acting without thinking recently, which is dangerous for someone as powerful as Barry. Barry is in the middle of a fight with [[Roy Bivolo]] (Rainbow Raider), [[Anthony Woodward]] (Girder) and [[Jeremy Tell]] (Double Down) when Hal steps in to help. Barry insists he hadn't needed Hal's help, but Hal comments that Batman ([[Bruce Wayne]]) had noted that Barry's Flash activity had gone up by 500%. When Barry tries to run away, Hal stops him and insists they talk instead to which Barry reluctantly agrees.

They get off the street and out of their costumes, heading over to Barry's apartment which is currently filled with mess, an uncharacteristic event for Barry. When Hal comments on this, Barry lashes out and insults Hal's commitment, to which Hal tries to deescalate and encourage Barry to discuss what's been bothering him. Before Barry can reply, a call comes through for the Flash to deal with villain [[Roscoe Dillon]], The Top. Hal and Barry both suit up and rush out to help.

When trying to handle Roscoe, Hal asks Barry why this might be happening. Barry says that Roscoe had been in hospital for the last while getting help with his psychiatric problems and that Barry had been visiting him up until he got busy. Hal suggests Barry and Roscoe talk it out, and puts them in a room construct.

Roscoe reveals that the doctor he had been seeing had passed away and that none of the other doctors at this facility were helping him, and that this event had been caused by his grief and now untreated psychiatric issues. Barry tells Roscoe that this isn't what his doctor would have wanted, to which Roscoe starts crying and turns himself in.

Barry and Hal are sat on a nearby building, and Barry finally starts to admit what has been happening. Barry had been feeling guilt over deaths of some co-workers of his at the CCPD that he had been unable to prevent. Hal comforts Barry, and insists that this is what friends are for, to talk about it.
